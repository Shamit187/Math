A Slow Reading Guide

Walking through every step of a graduate-level statistics textbook — the way it should have been written for first-time readers.

Source: R. W. Keener, Theoretical Statistics: Topics for a Core Course (Springer, 2010) 20 chapters · 557 pages

About this guide

Math textbooks tend to rush. They give you a definition, two theorems, and a proof before you've had time to ask what any of the symbols actually mean. This site is the opposite. We're going to take Keener's Theoretical Statistics and walk through it slowly — every definition unpacked, every "obvious" step spelled out, every bit of motivation that a textbook author thought was beneath them.

The book itself is excellent, but it assumes you already know what's worth caring about. We won't.

The promise

If you read a paragraph and don't understand it, that's a bug in this guide — not in you. Every paragraph should land. Tell me when one doesn't.

How to read this

Each chapter page mirrors a chapter of the book and is divided the same way. As you read, watch for these boxes:

Intuition

Pause

Common pitfall

Notation we'll use

Some symbols recur constantly. Memorize these now — it saves pain later.

  • $\mathcal{X}$ — a generic set (the "sample space" or universe). Read as script-X.
  • $\mathcal{A}$ — a collection of subsets of $\mathcal{X}$ (later: a $\sigma$-field).
  • $\mu$ — a measure (Greek "mu"). Assigns sizes to sets.
  • $P$ — a probability measure. A measure that happens to assign $1$ to the whole space.
  • $X, Y, Z$ — random variables (functions, not numbers).
  • $\sim$ — "is distributed as". $X \sim P$ means $X$ has distribution $P$.
  • $f, p$ — densities (the things that integrate to give probabilities).
  • $E[X]$ — expectation of $X$.
