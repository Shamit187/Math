About this book

Jonathan Haidt is a social psychologist who spent his career studying morality across cultures. This book distills that research into three core arguments about why humans are so morally divided — and why that division is unlikely to go away. It is a book about psychology first, politics second.

The writing is conversational and grounded in experiments. Each chapter opens with a story, builds through evidence, and lands on a principle. No prior knowledge of psychology or philosophy is needed.

The three parts

Part I — Intuitions Come First, Strategic Reasoning Second

We like to think moral judgment is rational deliberation. Haidt argues the opposite: we feel first, then reason backwards to justify the feeling. The "rational tail" wags only because the "intuitive dog" lets it.

Part II — There's More to Morality than Harm and Fairness

Western secular liberals think morality is basically about not hurting people and treating them fairly. But moral psychology shows six distinct "taste buds" — foundations that different cultures and political groups weight very differently. Conservatives use more of them.

Part III — Morality Binds and Blinds

Humans evolved as a "groupish" species — not fully selfish, not fully altruistic, but fiercely loyal to the team. Morality is the glue that holds groups together and the weapon they use against outsiders. Religion and politics exploit exactly this capacity.
